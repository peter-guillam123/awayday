Part 02 · Artificial intelligence

AI is moving between us and the facts.

As synthetic content floods the information ecosystem, risks move upstream into newsgathering and editing.

Primary sources

AI hoaxes: fake images, documents, quotes.

failure point 01

Verification

Synthetic consensus: repeated AI errors look corroborated.

failure point 02

Drafting / editing

Tool errors: hallucinated facts, bad summaries.

failure point 03

Publishing

Visible mistakes damage trust in the brand.

failure point 04

The response

Verified reporting, transparent sourcing, accountable editors, human-focused stories, direct audience relationships — both online and in person.

The choice: be quoted by platforms — or chosen directly by readers.

Part 02 · Artificial intelligence 09 / NN

Part 02 · Artificial intelligence

Beyond chat: the new AI stack.

01

The reasoning brain

Modern models have moved beyond word prediction. They plan approaches before generating a response. Recent models have blown through existing evaluations.

02

The harness

The harness is the execution environment. By giving models access to tools (search, terminal, CMS, browser, apps), AI moves from talking about work to doing it.

03

The agentic loop

The synergy of Brain + Harness creates an "Agent." It doesn't wait for your next prompt; it creates a plan, uses tools, and works in a loop until the goal is achieved.

Part 02 · Artificial intelligence 08 / NN

Part 02 · Artificial intelligence

AI is not just something that happens to us.

We are professionals with clear codes and practices that govern behaviour. And we can make judicious decisions about how we use AI in that frame.

01

Should I use AI?

This is entirely in your hands. But too often it's presented as the only element of control, and one that should be imposed on everyone.

02

Where should I use AI?

Your job is very likely an amalgam of a lot of jobs. Some of those are appropriate for AI and some absolutely depend on your personal control and expertise.

03

How should I use AI?

AI shouldn't - and doesn't have to - be used to execute a whole task. Instead of asking it to create something you can use it to give feedback and suggestions.

In practice

The project management of this group was run with an AI at every stage, from establishing timelines to writing key emails to generating every slide in this presentation. All of that integration made the process quicker and the output better. None of it removed or obscured the human expertise behind it.

Part 03 · External shifts

The paradox of the Trump bump.

Our US audience remains exceptionally strong during Trump's second term, significantly outpacing the Biden presidency in raw engagement.

Key challenge: volatility

Major news events still drive historic spikes, but audience interest is increasingly transient. Interest in core hard-news topics (e.g. Iran) now decays faster than in Trump 1.0, often dropping within 90 days.

Part 03 · External shifts 15 / NN

Part 03 · External shifts

What can we do about it?

Transitioning from platform dependence to direct ownership.

01

Diversify beyond politics

Build loyalty through consistent coverage of high-retention areas like climate and immigration. These topics sustain interest long after the transient "hard news" spikes fade.

02

Competitive lessons

Contrast the New York Times' multi-product success with the Washington Post's decline. Diversified lifestyle and utility content are essential for retaining Trump-era gains.

03

Own the audience

Counter the industry-wide decline in search and social referrals by prioritising direct channels: the app, homepage and newsletter products.

Part 03 · External shifts

Thinking about risk.

"A risk is a possibility of something bad happening. By their nature they're not easy to think about."

James Wallis, former Director of strategy, the Anthesis Group sustainability consultancy

Perceptions can be wrong

A unique challenge for a news organisation is that we view risks of threats to us through the filter of "the news".

Global threats, tiny operation

The Guardian has around 1,300 employees versus Nestlé's 271,000. It is hard to make space to think about risk.

Culture can block change, quietly

We need to understand both the obvious and the hidden things in our company culture that drive or block change.

Creativity is power

AI, climate destruction, authoritarianism… The feeling that they're all coming for us is disempowering. Allowing space for imagination and speculation creates agency.

Part 03 · External shifts 18 / NN

Part 03 · External shifts

Internal communication is essential.

Particularly now — as we head into a period of huge change we need to sharpen up on…

01

What we say — and what we think we have said.

Studies by Dr Boaz Keysar, expert and author in language, show that when you think you have been clear, and you are 85% sure that your message has been understood, about half of the people you were talking to completely missed the message.

There is a gap between what you think you said, and what they heard. Or we don't say anything at all and rely on information osmosis when we assume people know what we are thinking.

02

What we hear — and what we choose not to listen to.

We have a responsibility as listeners. Keysar says hearing what we want to hear can be a way to avoid having to take responsibility for things ourselves. When we are told a change is coming the response is often "Yes, but not to me".

Ambiguity causes all kinds of problems — and it can be costly.

$1T+

Businesses in the United States lose more than one trillion dollars a year to miscommunication, according to research by Grammarly and the Harris Poll.

$1.7B

Medical errors resulting from communication failures between caregivers cost $1.7 billion in malpractice claims and 2,000 preventable deaths in one study alone.

Group 4 · Workshop 22 / NN

Group activity

How it works.

01

Find your sheet

Quickly split into groups of six or seven. You'll find an A3 sheet on your table with three of the risks above already filled in. Each table has a different mix, so you don't all end up with the same ones. You'll have about 15 minutes to complete the task.

02

Sort and score

Talk through each risk together. Is it a News risk (a story we'll need to cover), an Organisational risk (a challenge to us as a business), or both? Then circle a probability and an impact — low, medium or high.

03

Three lines per risk

For each one, write three short lines: the specific risk to the Guardian, whether there's also an opportunity or upside, and how you think the Guardian can respond and make the best of it. We'll ask each group to share one key observation here, and we'll collate all the feedback and these slides into NotebookLM after the session.

Be clear, and ask to clarify. Try to be as clear as you can with each other, and don't hesitate to ask someone to clarify if you're not following. And think big. Assume the change you're imagining is doable — don't let practicality get in the way.

Appendix · AI adoption 37 / NN

Appendix · AI adoption

Easy for some. Hard for others.

Firms that have formally adopted

18%

Of US businesses report adopting AI in any business function (Fed BTOS, end 2025).

Workers using it anyway

41%

Of US workers use generative AI at work — often via personal accounts, ahead of their employer (Fed RPS).

Why deploying it is hard.

  • Identifying use cases What's flexible for one person becomes hard to define for a thousand. Personal use bends to the user; organisational use has to nail the use case first.
  • Reproducibility and audit trail A sole trader can let AI draft an invoice. A 1,000-person firm needs an output that survives an audit.
  • Error consequences at scale A hallucinated paragraph is a shrug in a personal email; a correction in a 200,000-circulation paper.
  • Workforce concerns about displacement Real, live conversations inside most large organisations — including ours.

Source: Federal Reserve, April 2026. The Fed also notes adoption among the smallest firms is "stronger than would be expected based on size alone".

Used widely. Trusted less.

Personal interest

Of global news audiences are interested in AI-generated summaries of news articles — the highest of any AI feature tested.

But trust falls

−18

Net score: AI-produced news is seen as less trustworthy than human-produced news, even with human oversight.

"Audiences remain broadly sceptical of these automated approaches to news production, but are more accepting of journalists using AI in a supportive role."

Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025 — the asymmetry of agency, in their words. People accept tools they control; they don't accept tools used on them.
